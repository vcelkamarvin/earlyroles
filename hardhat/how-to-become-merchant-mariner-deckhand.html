The route in, step by step

  1. Get the accredited tickets. Merchant Marine runs on safety certification — the required ones are listed below with real training providers.
  2. Pass the medical. Nearly every role needs a valid fitness-to-work medical.
  3. Build an offshore-format CV. Tickets, medicals and reliability first — not a white-collar layout.
  4. Apply through the right channel. These jobs come from crewing agencies and operator talent pools (e.g. Crowley Maritime, Faststream Marine), not general job boards.

Tickets & medicals

  • STCW Basic Training required · $900–$1,500 · 5 days — MITAGS
    Personal survival, fire, first aid, security — mandatory to work at sea.
  • Merchant Mariner Credential required · $140+ · 4–8 wks — USCG National Maritime Center
    US MMC (or flag-state seaman book) to sign on.
  • TWIC card required · $125 · 2–4 wks — TSA Universal Enrollment
    Required to board US vessels/terminals.
  • Merchant Marine Medical required · $100–$300 · 1 day — USCG-approved medical examiners
    Fitness & drug screen.

FAQ

How much does a merchant mariner / deckhand earn?

Entry pay is around $58k and experienced workers reach $130k. US mariners may qualify for foreign-earned income exclusion.

Can I become a merchant mariner / deckhand with no experience?

Yes. Merchant Marine has genuine entry routes — you qualify on accredited safety tickets and a medical, not on prior experience or a degree.

What tickets do I need to start?

The mandatory ones are: STCW Basic Training, Merchant Mariner Credential, TWIC card, Merchant Marine Medical. Training-provider links are listed above, free.
