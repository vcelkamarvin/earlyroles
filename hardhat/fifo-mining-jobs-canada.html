What these jobs actually are

Fly-in fly-out (FIFO) means you fly to site for the on-swing, work long shifts, sleep in camp — flights, a room and meals usually covered — then fly home for your off-swing. Around Fort McMurray, most entry hiring is for labourers, ground workers, safety watch and haul-truck operators, and a lot of it runs through contractors and staffing firms during turnaround (shutdown) season in spring and fall, not on LinkedIn.

The tickets you need (in order)
  • H2S Alive (Energy Safety Canada) — 1 day, valid 3 years. Non-negotiable for oil-sands sites.
  • CSO — Common Safety Orientation (or CSTS) — ~CAD 90, online, does not expire. This replaced the old OSSA BSO for site access.
  • Standard First Aid & CPR-C — most sites want it.
  • Class 1 or Class 3 licence — only if you want haul-truck / driving roles.

The test nobody warns you about

Almost every oil-sands site requires a pre-access drug & alcohol test plus a fit-for-duty check before you set foot on site. Cannabis is legal in Canada but not on site — a failed pre-access test ends the process. Plan for it.

Who hires (and how)

Producers like Suncor, CNRL and Imperial (Kearl) run the camps, but entry hiring often goes through contractors and staffing firms — the crewing agencies are where the entry roles actually come from. Get your tickets, build a Canadian-format CV, and get on their books ahead of turnaround season.

Straight talk: these entry camp jobs are, in practice, for Canadian citizens and permanent residents. If you're applying from abroad, read the honest answer on working Canada FIFO as a foreigner → before you spend a dollar.
